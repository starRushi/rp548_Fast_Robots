Equations Used

Conventions and units
Variables: \(a_x, a_y, a_z\) are accelerometer readings along the IMU axes. \( \omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z \) are gyroscope angular rates. Time step: \(dt\) in seconds. Angle units: compute in radians, convert to degrees for display. 
deg = rad * 180.0 / π
rad = deg * π / 180.0
Pitch and roll from accelerometer
Use accelerometer gravity direction to estimate tilt. These forms won't break while using \(\operatorname{atan2}\).
\[ \text{roll}_{acc} = \operatorname{atan2}(a_y, a_z) \] \[ \text{pitch}_{acc} = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(-a_x,\sqrt{a_y^2 + a_z^2}\right) \]
Implementation mapping: \(a_x = \texttt{accX}\), \(a_y = \texttt{accY}\), \(a_z = \texttt{accZ}\) (any consistent units, mg is fine).
Gyroscope angle integration (pitch, roll, yaw)
Gyroscope gives angular rate. Integrate over time to get angle. This drifts over time.
\[ \theta_g[k] = \theta_g[k-1] + \omega[k]\;dt \]
First order low-pass filter (used on accelerometer angle or raw accel)
Smooth noisy signals before fusion or for plotting.
\[ y[k] = \alpha\,y[k-1] + (1-\alpha)\,x[k] \] \[ \tau = \frac{1}{2\pi f_c} \qquad \alpha = \frac{\tau}{\tau + dt} \]
\(f_c\) is the cutoff frequency in Hz chosen from your FFT analysis.
Complementary filter (accelerometer plus gyroscope)
Combines gyro short-term stability with accel long-term reference.
\[ \theta[k] = \beta\left(\theta[k-1] + \omega[k]\;dt\right) + (1-\beta)\,\theta_{acc}[k] \]
\(\beta\) is typically close to 1 (example: 0.95 to 0.99). Use the same for the other two.
FFT frequency axis (for your Python spectrum plot)
Used to label the spectrum and justify \(f_c\). If your sampling rate is \(f_s\) and you collect \(N\) samples:
\[ f_s \approx \frac{1}{\operatorname{median}(\Delta t)} \qquad f[i] = \frac{i\,f_s}{N}, \; i = 0,1,\dots,\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor \]
